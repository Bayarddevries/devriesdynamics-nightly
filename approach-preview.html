I start with nothing. I make something that matters.

Every project here began with a blank page, no budget, and a problem someone needed solved. I build the plane while flying it — and I've gotten very good at it.

Three project narratives — full PSR format

Shoebox V2

Turning fading memories into a searchable archive

Problem: Métis history was literally fading in shoeboxes. Stories lost as generations passed.

Role: Solo build — grassroots organizing, scanning, metadata, frontend, training.

Challenge: No budget, no team, no process. Every photo needed a family, a story, and a place.

Outcome: Community members now find relatives and reconnect with history they thought was lost.

Homeland Map

Mapping a homeland that history tried to erase

Problem: No interactive way to show how far-reaching Métis influence was.

Role: Solo research and build — learned Google Earth Pro, compiled GeoJSON, built the viz.

Challenge: Multiple historical maps needing consensus. No spatial research background.

Outcome: Researchers validated the approach. Users explore a history that was almost lost.

RRMNHC Site

A testbed that became the product

Problem: Leadership needed to see concepts before committing to expensive dev.

Role: Designer + AI agent orchestrator. Rapid prototyping at production quality.

Challenge: Looking world-class without a design team. Every iteration had to be polished.

Outcome: Prototype became the production site. Method is now repeatable across projects.

Pros

  • Full PSR (Problem → Role → Challenge → Outcome) structure
  • Distinct from generic portfolio — feels like a real case study document
  • Impressive to anyone scrolling a hiring manager's stack

Cons

  • More implementation work
  • New nav item to manage
  • Could feel heavy if overdone

My recommendation: Start with Approach C as a dedicated page — it's the strongest signal for a senior ops/leadership role. Add Approach A's card descriptions as a complement.
